What Anthropic’s usage research can—and cannot—tell founders

Claude conversations reveal patterns in AI use. Turning those patterns into a product strategy requires care about the sample, the task and the outcome.

Start with what was actually measured

A claim about how people use one AI product is not automatically a claim about the whole economy. That distinction makes Anthropic’s Economic Index useful to read carefully. Its September 2025 report examines how Claude usage differs across tasks, geographies and enterprise API deployments.

The September report describes coding as a major area of use and a shift toward more task delegation on Claude.ai. It reports automation patterns in 77% of the business API use it studied, compared with about half of Claude.ai use. Those are findings about the company’s observed traffic, not a census of all workers or proof of job displacement. [Source: Anthropic ↗]

Collaboration can be a product feature

Our interpretation: founders should resist assuming that every successful AI product must remove the person from the workflow. A tool that helps a user revise a draft, understand a result or compare options can have a coherent purpose. The important question is whether the interaction improves a real task enough for somebody to keep using it.

Imagine a product that prepares a customer response. It might be designed to send automatically, suggest a draft for review or explain the reasoning behind a recommendation. Each design needs a different evaluation. Measuring speed alone could miss whether the user trusts the suggestion, makes substantial corrections or abandons it.

Activity is not the same as impact

Conversation counts are informative about engagement, but they do not directly establish productivity, revenue or job losses. A long conversation can represent productive iteration or difficulty getting an acceptable answer. Frequent use may reflect high value, a difficult task or both.

For a startup, a stronger evidence plan would connect a clear task to an observable outcome. Track whether users finish the job, how much review they need and whether they return. Where possible, compare results with the previous workflow under similar conditions. Keep a record of errors that would matter to the customer, rather than only showcasing successful examples.

Read provider research with two lenses

The first lens is descriptive: what does the report actually observe, in which product and during which period? The second is strategic: what hypothesis does that suggest for your own users? Moving from one to the other should trigger a test, not a sweeping conclusion.

Anthropic’s research gives builders a vocabulary for thinking about assistance and automation. It does not decide which experience a startup should ship. That decision depends on the task, the cost of mistakes and the amount of control customers want to retain.
